Imagine you are trying to predict something, like how high your electricity bill will be based on the temperature outside, or how well you'll score on an exam based on the hours you studied.

Simple Linear Regression is just a math tool that lets you draw a straight line through your data points to make those kinds of predictions.

The Main Idea
You have two things you are looking at:
    X (The Independent Variable): The thing you already know or can control (e.g., hours studied).

    Y (The Dependent Variable): The thing you are trying to guess or predict (e.g., your exam score). Your score depends on how much you studied.


Y = b_0 + b_1X_1

    This is just the classic equation for a straight line 
    (you might remember $y = mx + c$ from school), broken into parts:
    Y: The prediction you want to find.
    
    b_0 (The Intercept): The starting point. This is what Y would be if X was exactly zero.
     For example, if you study for 0 hours, what is your baseline score
     
     b_1 (The Slope / Coefficient): The multiplier. This tells you how much Y changes every time $X$ goes up by 1. If $b_1$ is 5, it means every extra hour you study adds 5 points to your score.
     
     X_1: The actual value you plug into the formula (e.g., 4 hours of studying).



OLS(ordinary least square):
    perfect best-fit line
    Think of OLS as a game of trying to draw a line that gets as close as possible to all your data points at once.

    What is a "Residual"? (The Error)
    When you use your formula to predict sales based on an advertising budget, your prediction won't always be 100% perfect.
     There will be a gap between reality and your guess.y_i (Actual Value): The real sales you actually made in the past.
    
    
     hat{y}_i (Predicted Value): The sales value your straight line thinks you made
     
     .Residual (E_i): The error gap between them (y_i - hat{y}_i).
     In plain English: If you actually sold 100 units (y_i),
 
      but your line predicted you'd sell 95 units (hat{y}_i), your residual (error) is $100 - 95 = 5$.

2. Why "Least Squares"?
    If you just add up all the errors, positive errors (line guessed too low) and negative errors (line guessed too high)
    will cancel each other out. To fix this, we square every single error (which makes them all positive numbers).

The term Residual Sum of Squares (RSS) just means: "Square all the errors, and add them all up."


3. What does OLS actually do?
    The slide says: "OLS minimizes the Residual Sum of Squares (RSS)"This means OLS is an algorithm that tweaks the starting point ($b_0$)
    and the slope ($b_1$) of your line over and over again until the total squared error is as small (minimum) as humanly possible.
