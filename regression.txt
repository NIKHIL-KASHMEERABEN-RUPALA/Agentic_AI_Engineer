Imagine you are trying to predict something, like how high your electricity bill will be based on the temperature outside, or how well you'll score on an exam based on the hours you studied.

Simple Linear Regression is just a math tool that lets you draw a straight line through your data points to make those kinds of predictions.

The Main Idea
You have two things you are looking at:
    X (The Independent Variable): The thing you already know or can control (e.g., hours studied).

    Y (The Dependent Variable): The thing you are trying to guess or predict (e.g., your exam score). Your score depends on how much you studied.


Y = b_0 + b_1X_1

    This is just the classic equation for a straight line 
    (you might remember $y = mx + c$ from school), broken into parts:
    Y: The prediction you want to find.
    
    b_0 (The Intercept): The starting point. This is what Y would be if X was exactly zero.
     For example, if you study for 0 hours, what is your baseline score
     
     b_1 (The Slope / Coefficient): The multiplier. This tells you how much Y changes every time $X$ goes up by 1. If $b_1$ is 5, it means every extra hour you study adds 5 points to your score.
     
     X_1: The actual value you plug into the formula (e.g., 4 hours of studying).
